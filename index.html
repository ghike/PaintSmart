Main UX gapsWhat creates friction today
06

1. Onboarding Clarity

  • PaintSmart has no effective initial onboarding: the user lands directly in the product without context, orientation or a clear first action.
  • The issue goes beyond first access: the product also lacks feature onboarding, so users must infer how core objects and workflows are supposed to work.

As a result, the system exposes structure before it creates understanding.

Main UX gapsWhat creates friction today
07

2. Workflow (Lead → Customer)

  • PaintSmart does not have a real lead layer: every new prospect is created directly as a customer, so the product does not distinguish between an early-stage lead and an actual client
  • This makes the customer model heavier than it should be: users are forced to treat every contact as a fully formed customer, even when the relationship is still pre-sale or unqualified.
  • A lead/customer separation might make more sense: the product could keep the current proposal/job structure, while adding a lighter lead stage before the customer record is created.

As a result, the system exposes structure before it creates understanding.

Main UX gapsWhat creates friction today
08

3. Visibility

  • PaintSmart has setup progress, but not feature onboarding: the product gives the user a company configuration checklist, but it does not guide them through the core features afterward.
  • The current onboarding stops too early: once the company setup is done, the app seems to drop the user into the interface without teaching how to actually use the product.
  • This makes the app harder to adopt: the user may complete configuration but still not understand what to do next or how each feature fits into the workflow.

As a result, the current experience shows the interface to the user, but lacks providing enough guidance and helping them seeing value in the app.

Main UX gapsWhat creates friction today
09

4. Continuity (Proposal → Job)

  • The proposal-to-job transition is not clearly communicated: when a proposal is signed, it becomes a job, but the system does not seem to give a visible success message or a clear confirmation of the change.
  • This makes the state change feel abrupt: the user may not immediately understand that the proposal was converted and the job now exists as a new stage in the workflow.
  • The historical context is not preserved well enough: once the proposal becomes a job, the user loses easy access to the original proposal history and the signature context.

As a result, this makes harder for users to know what became a job and how the proposal was approved and what happened before execution.

Core product tensionPositioning vs product reality
10
Key observation

The current message and the current system do not fully point to the same user.

When the brand says “self-employed painter”, the expected product is simpler, faster and more assistance-driven. When the product behaves like an FSM, users expect clearer CRM structure, scheduling logic, negotiation support and operational continuity.

What this means for the product strategy?

If the target is self-employed

Reduce complexity, guide the first win quickly, support ad-hoc lead intake, simplify daily planning and separate owner life from company operations.

If the target is small teams

Lean into FSM logic, improve job management, calendar visibility, status clarity, crew coordination and a stronger customer record.

Current state

The product sits between those two worlds, which creates friction in both messaging and usability.

Recommendation

Choose the primary ICP first, then adjust onboarding, roadmap and marketing around that decision.

Functional gaps for the ICPEspecially for self-employed users
11

Lead handling

The product would benefit from a lightweight way to capture and qualify leads before forcing them into a client/job structure.

Negotiation support

There is little support for the real commercial back-and-forth that happens before a proposal becomes accepted work.

Calendar integration

For a self-employed painter, simple visibility into schedule may matter more than heavy workforce management.

Financial reality of self-employed workers

Self-employed users often mix individual and business finances. There is an opportunity to support a simpler operating model, rather than only a company-style management structure.

Product direction

  • Strategic framing: the current scope fits an FSM direction better than a purely self-employed workflow.
  • Roadmap implication: the chosen ICP should drive how much the product is simplified or expanded.

What PaintSmart can learn from JobberAdoptable patterns
13

More explicit onboarding

Jobber sets expectations earlier and helps users understand the path to value faster.

Stronger customer record

A more complete client view makes commercial history, billing and communication easier to understand in one place.

Marketing-to-product consistency

Its landing page, calls to action and trial design work together to push the user toward activation.

Opportunity for PaintSmart

Surface setup progress earlier, explain object relationships more clearly and connect the landing page promise to the very first in-app actions.

Important nuance

The takeaway is not “copy Jobber”. It is “borrow what improves clarity and trust without losing PaintSmart’s own direction”.

What PaintSmart can learn from JobberCognitive Bias
14

1. Bandwagoning through social proof

  • Jobber applies bandwagoning from the first screen: ratings, review counts, and customer imagery quickly signal market validation.
  • This builds confidence before explanation: the visitor feels that the product is already trusted by others in the same industry.
  • PaintSmart could use stronger social proof earlier: testimonials, review volume, and visible customer adoption would help reduce friction at first contact.

Jobber does not wait to explain the product first, but to first show that many others already trust it.

What PaintSmart can learn from JobberCognitive Bias
15

2. Zero-Risk through Trials

  • Jobber reduces perceived risk from the first screen: the free-trial framing makes adoption feel easier and safer.
  • This lowers commitment anxiety: the user feels they can explore the product before making a stronger decision.
  • PaintSmart could make first use feel safer too: clearer onboarding and faster visible value would reduce hesitation at the start.

Jobber does not ask the user to commit first, but first makes the product feel safe to try.

What PaintSmart can learn from JobberCognitive Bias
16

3. Creating urgency to act (FOMO)

  • Jobber uses FOMO through time-sensitive messaging: limited offers create a reason to act now instead of later.
  • This reduces decision inertia: the user feels that delaying may mean losing a benefit already available.
  • PaintSmart could introduce softer urgency cues: activation prompts and time-based incentives could help increase early engagement.

Jobber does not only explain why the product matters, but also gives the user a reason to act now.

What PaintSmart can learn from JobberOptimized SEO
17

4. Optimized SEO

  • Jobber uses SEO to win high-intent comparison traffic: competitor pages help it appear when buyers search for alternatives.
  • This supports both discoverability and positioning: the brand is found at the exact moment users are comparing options.
  • PaintSmart could replicate this with comparison-led content: SEO pages focused on alternatives and competitor trade-offs could strengthen acquisition.

Jobber does not rely only on brand discovery, but also intercepts users who are already comparing tools.

What PaintSmart can learn from WorkeverOperational learnings
18

Operational breadth

Workever shows how an FSM can support jobs, forms, scheduling and field execution more broadly.

Trade-off

More breadth can also create longer, heavier flows. This is useful because it shows what PaintSmart should avoid while it grows.

Key takeaway

PaintSmart can expand operationally, but it should protect simplicity and avoid overwhelming the first-time user.

Recommendation

Adopt only the Workever patterns that improve job execution, visibility and continuity. Do not import complexity that weakens the self-employed or small-team experience.

Priority recommendationsWhat to do next
19

1. Define the primary ICP

Choose whether PaintSmart is first for self-employed painters or for small field teams. This decision should drive roadmap and messaging.

2. Make the first-use path obvious

Bring setup progress forward, clarify the first key action and explain how proposals, clients and jobs relate.

3. Preserve continuity

Keep commercial and operational history connected after a proposal becomes a job, so users never lose context.

4. Strengthen the self-employed workflow

Add lighter lead capture, negotiation support and simpler schedule visibility if the chosen ICP remains self-employed professionals.

5. Align product and brand

Landing page, onboarding, feature emphasis and value proposition should all tell the same story about who the product is for and why it wins.
